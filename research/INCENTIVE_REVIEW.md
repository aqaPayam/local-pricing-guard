## What this answers

This study closes a real interpretive gap: a coordinated feasible path that
minimizes customer benefit need not be attractive to either individual seller.
The opponent is fixed to the known public upper-price script; the focal seller
optimizes within exactly the same numerical masks and cap phases. A positive
gain rejects that particular script's unilateral stability at the stated
state/discount. It neither refutes the all-policy customer-benefit floor nor
demonstrates learning, equilibrium selection or autonomous collusion.

The method is established finite-state dynamic programming. The close 2026
frozen-policy unilateral audit already recorded in REFINEMENT_NOVELTY.md must
be cited. A periodic reset simplifies this mechanism to exact finite-block
optimization; this is useful execution, not a new general best-response method.

## Actual findings, including the simple explanation

Every one of the eight games in both recurring-period slots has a strictly
positive last-guard one-shot gain for each firm. This elementary screen alone
rejects the script's best-response stability from that phase for every discount
0<=gamma<1. From cap activation it gives a positive gain for every gamma>0.
The next real cap resets both quotes, so the profitable late deviation needs
no conjecture about future punishment. The full dynamic program measures the
best attainable gain rather than discovering the qualitative rejection.

The magnitudes are small. Base15 selected K4 at gamma .95 improves a seller's
infinite discounted profit by approximately 0.41755, about 0.0896% of its baseline
value. K16 gives approximately 0.61728 or 0.1293%. Across the recurring slot/game
comparisons, relative gains are about 0.0797%–0.2163%. These are exact positive
signs for the saved numerical game, not evidence that the improvement would be
economically material or robust to unmodeled frictions.

Permanent cap has zero deviation gain in seven of eight games. The inherited
base11 cap permits a small improvement: about 0.00533% at gamma .95. It should
not be presented as an exception to an exact competitive-cap theorem: the old
physical cap is a rounded inherited grid quote, not a fresh exact Nash price.
Both firms and the gamma0 diagnostics are retained rather than averaged away.

The independent finite certificates find that all eight continuous-selected K
values also meet their original 95% thresholds in their own finite games;
this is a checked result, not an assumed transfer. Fixed K16 meets that target
in only three of eight games. A profitable focal deviation remains feasible
under the same masks, so none of these incentive findings invalidates the
controller's policy-independent customer-benefit certificate.

There is no evidence here of incentive-compatible adverse equilibrium, policy
learning, empirical collusion, uncertain enforcement or a new general algorithm.
The useful conclusion is that safety should not be sold as a prediction that
self-interested sellers will follow the most adverse feasible script. Equally,
the script's instability does not make a worst-case safety guarantee unnecessary.
My final informal execution judgment is 4–4.5/5, clarity about 4/5 and
methodological originality about 3/5 for the combined narrow program.
These are developer judgments, not hackathon scores or a ranking prediction.
