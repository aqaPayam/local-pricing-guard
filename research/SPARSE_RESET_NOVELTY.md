# Recurring competitive caps: primary-source novelty review

Reviewed 26 October 2026, initially against the historical Nash-cap candidate and now aligned to the approved inherited-physical-cap mechanism in `SPARSE_RESET_PROTOCOL.md` and `SPARSE_RESET_THEORY.md`. References and source-reading scope are unchanged. `SPARSE_RESET_CANDIDATE.md` remains a provisional historical draft, not the accepted controller specification. Literature and analytic positioning only: no new market calculations, simulations, learners or API players were run. This note is outside the frozen one-time activation and refinement studies. The approved protocol/theory specify the ongoing study; completed numerical claims still require the corresponding independent audit receipts. This literature note is not such a receipt.

## Assessment

Periodic resetting, alternating controlled/uncontrolled periods, and trading intervention frequency against performance are established ideas. Averaging the rewards of a repeated deterministic cycle is elementary. The proposed cap schedule is therefore not a new control paradigm, and the `1/K` comparison is not by itself a substantial novelty claim.

The narrower potentially useful result is an economic, all-permitted-policy calculation: in this explicitly bounded, known, symmetric logit market, recurring executions of the exact inherited original physical cap admit a tight scalar upper-envelope calculation of worst long-run consumer surplus under the stated condition A_cap < q_star. The cap price is held fixed when timing changes; it is not silently moved to continuous Nash or rounded to a different mesh. That calculation can turn a fixed target into a largest admissible period within one specified family, for targets above the unchanged continuous guard floor, for which finite initialization cannot guarantee the target after control is relinquished. This is a conditional, model-specific synthesis of established ideas. The targeted search below does not establish that nobody has derived the same special case.

## Six targeted primary sources

1. **Benjamin De Bruyne and Francesco Mori (2023), “Resetting in Stochastic Optimal Control,” Physical Review Research 5, 013122.** [Primary full text, arXiv v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.11416v4); [bibliographic/version record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.11416); [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevResearch.5.013122). Inspected the state/reset model, payoff functional and optimal reset region in equations (3)–(7), discounted example and extensions in equation (11) and Appendix E. The paper already optimizes reset policies against reward and reset costs, including discrete-time extensions. Its stochastic, state-dependent controller is more general than a fixed public clock in some respects. It does not supply this candidate's adversarial seller-policy quantifier or cap-set calculation. Do not call our frequency tradeoff the first optimization of restarting, or infer that fixed-period caps are globally optimal compared with state-dependent resets.

2. **Arnob Ray, Arnab Pal, Dibakar Ghosh, Syamal K. Dana and Chittaranjan Hens (2020 preprint), “Mitigating long transient time in deterministic systems by resetting.”** [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2007.08642v1); [submission record and related publication DOI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08642). Inspected the deterministic model, spatial control-line projection, exponential versus sharp reset protocols, frequency scans, and Supplemental Section VII equations (19)–(20). Periodic restarting of deterministic systems and optimization of its period already occur here. The metric is transient time to a target in oscillator/Lorenz systems, rather than worst infinite-average customer benefit in an adversarial market. Their numerical advantage of an optimal sharp reset does not establish optimality of our periodic schedule. Our intervention changes actual market executions in a cap set, rather than projecting an oscillator to a chosen line. The DOI landing page was inaccessible in this browsing session, so no unverified journal volume/page details are asserted here.

3. **Stephan Eule and Jakob J. Metzger (2016), “Non-equilibrium steady states of stochastic processes with intermittent resetting,” New Journal of Physics 18, 033006.** [Primary author preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1510.07876); [journal article and DOI](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/18/3/033006). Inspected the renewal construction and generalized evolution equation, the deterministic linear-motion example, and the deterministic-reset limit of the waiting-time distribution. The long-run occupation statistics depend on the full reset schedule, including regular resetting, and not simply its mean frequency. This is a warning against treating one period's result as a universal frequency-only theorem. The paper's stochastic renewal model differs from our public fixed-phase, worst-policy cap. A predictable deterministic cycle needs no new renewal theorem to justify its arithmetic average; the economically relevant work is proving the envelope is both a lower guarantee and attainable worst behavior.

4. **Jitai Liang and Wanjun Xia (2020), “Global exponential stabilization of nonlinear systems via width time-dependent periodically intermittent smooth control,” Advances in Difference Equations 2020, 576.** [Primary journal full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13662-020-02969-3). Inspected equations (2.1)–(2.3), work/rest widths, Definition 2.1 and Theorem 3.1's Lyapunov conditions. Periodic control windows, time without control and admissible control-width constraints are already explicit design variables. Their objective is exponential stability of nonlinear dynamics, with feedback active during positive-duration work windows, rather than a consumer-surplus average when sellers choose arbitrary permitted actions. Our discrete cap round has real duration and reward: it must not be described as a free instantaneous reset. Neither our cap nor a target average automatically stabilizes all price trajectories. No priority claim about intermittent control is justified.

5. **Michael J. Neely (2021), “Fast Learning for Renewal Optimization in Online Task Scheduling,” Journal of Machine Learning Research 22(279), 1–44.** [Official record](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v22/20-813.html); [primary full text](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume22/20-813/20-813.pdf). Inspected Section 1's reward/time ratio, equation (1), and the optimization formulation (2)–(4). Optimizing long-run reward per duration over repeated frames is established. That paper learns unknown task distributions and proves convergence rates; our candidate assumes a fully known deterministic economic model and does not inherit those learning guarantees. A fixed-length cycle's total consumer benefit divided by its length is a basic instance of this accounting. The candidate's potential contribution comes from evaluating the adverse choices within the frame, not inventing reward-per-time optimization or a new learning algorithm.

6. **Gianluca Brero, Eric Mibuari, Nicolas Lepore and David C. Parkes (2022), “Learning to Mitigate AI Collusion on Economic Platforms,” NeurIPS 35.** [Official proceedings full text](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/f746974abd33c0015ca583a267dac1fd-Paper-Conference.pdf). Rechecked Sections 2–5, including continuous logit consumer surplus, threshold platform rules, the behavioral Stackelberg objective, and robustness against different seller behavior/costs. Designing ongoing platform commitments for consumer benefit is already the economic objective. Their intervention controls buy-box inclusion and is optimized against modeled learning behavior; ours proposes periodic actual-price caps and a worst-permitted-policy certificate in a known market. These differences do not establish superior consumer welfare, practical implementability, or real-market robustness. Restarting Q-learning between their experimental episodes is not the same object as periodically executing our cap in a single ongoing market.

## Established ingredients versus the possible addition

| Ingredient | Conservative status |
|---|---|
| Repeat an intervention every K steps | Established periodic/intermittent control; not our invention. |
| Charge a frequency of 1/K | Accounting within the declared clocked family; not a general measure of autonomy or economic intervention cost. |
| Evaluate a repeated cycle by total reward/K | Elementary cycle/renewal-reward accounting. |
| Show Cesàro averages tend to the limiting reward | Standard convergence fact; not a new mathematical mechanism. |
| Bound the remaining geometric transient using a contraction | Standard tool. Its assumptions for the actual economic upper allowance must be verified. |
| Prove every permitted seller path is bounded by a tight scalar economic envelope | Potential useful model-specific lemma, inherited from and dependent on the reviewed continuous guard theory. |
| Derive exact G_K and the largest admissible K for a consumer target | Potential narrow economic design result within one inherited common physical-cap, fixed-period family; requires validated comparisons and boundary cases. |
| Claim optimal sparse intervention in general | Unsupported: adaptive clocks, asymmetric interventions, different cap levels and random schedules are unexamined. |

## Suggested contribution wording

> Under the stated bounded symmetric logit and contraction assumptions, we analyze one fixed-period family that repeatedly executes the same inherited physical price cap, with the original guard between cap rounds. A tight upper-price envelope gives the worst long-run consumer-surplus cycle average against every permitted seller policy, and therefore characterizes the largest fixed period meeting a specified target within that family. This extends the one-time-handover analysis to a persistent intervention; it does not introduce periodic resetting or establish optimality over general intervention policies.

Until validated outputs exist, replace “gives” and “characterizes” with “we propose to compute” when describing completed work. An independently reviewed analytic theorem and a validated numerical implementation should be reported separately.

## Issues to settle before a subsequent study

The crucial proof obligation is tightness, not only an upper bound on prices: each cap execution must permit the declared ceiling pair (p_cap, p_cap) and each following upper choice must be feasible under the same controller. The rule replaces the guard during the cap round, rather than silently requiring an incompatible intersection of two restrictions. Both shops retain arbitrary choices inside the permitted sets. A first partial cycle and any initial phase contribute a finite transient to the infinite average, but matter to every finite-horizon promise.

The protocol should compare permanent caps, no recurring cap, and the same guard under the same nominal/effective tolerance and imported real constants. It should retain targets already met by the guard, targets above the same physical cap’s maximum-price-pair benefit, equality/straddler cases and cases where the sufficient theorem is unresolved. The normalized cap A_cap=(p_cap-c)/mu must retain its physical source identity, with the stated order/contraction assumptions and A_cap < q_star checked. An interval-certified G_K result should be accompanied by the worst individual round and the number of cap executions, without treating these as interchangeable objectives. No seller-incentive or equilibrium claim follows from the permitted adverse path.

The separate finite human game uses its saved 61-price base mesh with the exact inherited cap quote prioritized over any base quote within the predeclared 1e-12 price distance, followed by sorting and exact deduplication (61/62 choices), with newly computed guard masks and its own exact rational certificate for saved numerical reward weights. That certificate is distinct from the continuous validated result, even though the physical cap is the same. The cap activation frequency 1/K is also distinct from realized quote-change frequency.

The most informative further extension would ask whether a state-dependent intervention can certify the same target with fewer actual restrictions than the fixed clock. That is a distinct optimization problem, not a claim supported by the current candidate. It would need a careful intervention-cost definition, information assumptions and a new protocol rather than a favorable illustrative trajectory.

This targeted review uses six primary sources and does not exhaust periodic-reset, impulse-control or economic-regulation literature. It supports cautious positioning, not an originality guarantee.
