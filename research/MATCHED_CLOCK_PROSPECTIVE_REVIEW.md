## Initial verdict

The proposed common five-phase design is a consequential control. It removes
unequal numbers of public clock labels and allocated Q cells from the narrow
comparison. Both arms start at a one-time cap phase0, then share the cycle
1,2,3,4,1. The recurring arm caps at phase4; startup uses its guard at every
cyclic phase. Both retain full completed price history even at a cap. Recurring
cap executions are1,5,9,..., and phase0 must never return.

With t completed executions, the NEXT phase is0 at t=0 and
1+((t-1) mod4) at t>0. Both1M and2M checkpoints consequently have NEXT phase4.
A continuing recurring-policy evaluation starts with a legitimate scheduled cap;
startup evaluation starts with its ordinary phase4 guard. Giving startup a new
activation cap, forcing both starts to phase1, or evaluating a preceding phase
would change the predeclared intervention and endpoint.

Both sellers in both arms allocate the same dense five-phase by own-history by
rival-history by action arrays, with zero initialization. At n=62 this is
1,191,640 binary64 Q cells per firm. Selection and NEXT-state bootstrapping
still use each arm's actual legal mask; forbidden allocated cells remain zero
and unvisited. Equal allocation does not create equal effective legal choices,
visitation, reward histories, learned competence or convergence. Those changed
experiences are mechanisms of ongoing enforcement, not nuisance variables that
can be retrospectively made equal.

This control does not identify how much of the OLD treatment difference was
caused by clock capacity. It would require a representation-by-enforcement
factorial to decompose that old difference. The narrow new claim is a comparison
under one matched clock and allocated architecture, conditional on its fixed
