The experiment at a glance

These are two guard-tolerance conditions in the same economic calibration. Different initial scores and checkpoints are sensitivity checks, not new economies. The study tests behavior under these rules; it has no unrestricted learner-capability comparison.

How learning and evaluation differ

During training, both firms choose before either current choice is revealed. Each updates only its own chosen score: Q ← Q + 0.1 × [own expected profit + 0.95 × best permitted next score − Q]. The next legal set includes the actual cap/guard clock. A cap is not a terminal state for learning.

The state includes the phase and both previous prices, including at a cap. A cap resets allowed prices; learned choices may still depend on history. Frozen evaluation keeps the actual final history and clock. The one-startup-cap condition receives no extra cap.

Expected profits come from the saved demand model; no customers or reward noise are sampled. Numerical learning uses ordinary floating-point arithmetic. Exact frozen-cycle quantities are rational calculations on the saved numerical reward weights.

What the evidence can tell us

Changing the cap rhythm also changes the number of phase-dependent score rows. Equal rounds do not provide equal visits per row. The starting scores, fixed exploration schedule, two-million-round budget and legal masks all affect behavior.

Some off-path greedy states may use default scores that were never trained. Even the eventual continuation can differ from occupied training behavior. Visit support is shown explicitly; frozen graph cycles do not prove training convergence or strategic stability.
