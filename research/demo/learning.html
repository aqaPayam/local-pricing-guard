Protection has a price.

A guard can improve customer benefit by replacing the seller’s choices. Compare it with direct best response, fixed competitive pricing, and a simple price cap before attributing value to a new method.

Retaining a proposed action is a measure of intervention burden. It does not by itself show that useful business competence or long-run profit is preserved.

What this does—and does not—answer

This study tests learned price proposals, policy transfer, information resets, and action constraints in one small market. The language-based handover question remains open: Q tables cannot interpret notes. Wrong-model, unilateral, released-guard, and sensitivity cases are retained rather than discarded.

Optional three-firm, unequal-product, demand-shock and timing checks use numerical proposal rules; they are not additional learned-agent replications. Human usability has developer checks; independent human comprehension remains unverified.
