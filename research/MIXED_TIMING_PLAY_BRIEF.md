# Prospective graphical explanation: when should the cap return?

Design brief only, outside V9. No accepted numerical result or implemented game
is implied. Build only after the mixed-timing protocol's independent evidence
gate. Preserve all previous game executors and reports.

Presentation default, selected before new case execution: finite
base-g15-t1 / cap-95pct, mixed calendar. Retain this default even if its outcome
is zero, unavailable or visually uninteresting. This is an interface choice,
not a scientifically preregistered experimental factor or an outcome selection.

Prospective main-report figure: show cap-95pct for all eight scenarios in all
three populations (finite, effective continuous, nominal continuous). Compare
relative cap-frequency reduction against each record's own certified best
fixed K; continuous points show constructive reduction and certified bounds.
Unavailable/startup-only/censored rows get explicit status text, never a zero
point in place of an undefined comparison. Full 40/80-row tables and complete
status counts remain accessible. This focuses the figure on the existing
95% reference question without selecting the best-looking target after results.

The opening should explain one concrete problem: a cap helps customers now,
but the guard can permit gradually higher prices afterwards. A fixed rhythm
may cap slightly more often than needed. Can a longer gap be funded safely
using benefit guaranteed by earlier rounds?

Keep the two existing illustrated shops and manual price controls. The user
knowingly chooses both shops' prices; no hidden AI player or experimental
account call is introduced. The current rule is prominently CAP or GUARD.
The same physical cap and legal guard sets apply. A real execution, never old
memory or a future block summary, earns the displayed reward.

Show three different quantities with explicit names:

1. Customer benefit actually earned by the chosen executed prices and its
   running average since activation.
2. The target line for that exact saved finite numerical game.
3. A guaranteed buffer computed from the certified worst permitted prices.
   This is a proof ledger, not cash, a refund or the user's actual profit.

A calendar graphic should show caps separated by short or long gaps. Explain
the next decision in plain language: a longer gap is available only after the
guaranteed buffer can fund its possible loss. Previous credit is retained
across caps. Extra benefit from cooperative manual prices does not change this
particular conservative calendar. State that the guarantee covers every prefix
from the first cap; individual rounds or windows can fall below the target.

Compare the mixed calendar with the same record's best fixed integer period,
using exact signs and a clear asymptotic-rate label. A short replay need not
match that limiting frequency. When the first long gap is far beyond the
preview, show that honestly; do not skip ahead and award unexecuted rewards.
Keep finite exact certificates separate from conservative continuous rate
intervals. Zero, tiny positive, unavailable, unresolved and censored states
must have different displays.

Before delivery, independently check all exported cases, actual rule timing,
exact funding ties, action legality, cumulative reward, a partial final gap,
reset/selection behavior and fail-closed evidence loading. Inspect real desktop
and narrow layouts, keyboard controls, readable shop graphics and chart scroll
behavior. Describe these as developer QA, never a human usability study.

## Proposed finite-game executor boundary

After evidence acceptance, a new pure executor can accept only a validated
finite record and its certified target/calendar. Do not alter recurring-game.js
or drive old phase-Q tables with the new credit clock. Store the full actual
previous price pair, completed executions, completed-block credit, position
inside the chosen gap and exact accumulated actual reward. Announce the current
mode before collecting either manual quote, then settle both simultaneously.
Supports use the previous rival quote, never a newly moved current slider.

At a block boundary choose its gap from the exact funded-credit comparison.
No credit or profit is awarded merely for choosing the gap. The displayed
guaranteed buffer during a partial finite block is completed-block credit plus
the exact adverse-envelope reward sum of its already executed prefix minus F
times that prefix length. At the block end this equals the updated boundary
credit. Keep that lower guarantee separate from the actual chosen-price
customer-benefit excess. Independently require actual excess >= guaranteed
buffer >= 0 at every settlement. A reset earns nothing and starts with one
pending real cap. A startup-only certificate has that cap and then guard
forever. Infeasible evidence or a record without a certified schedule cannot
unlock certified manual play. An unresolved/censored optimum with a separately
certified fixed-safe fallback may unlock that fallback, explicitly labeled as
safe without claiming its frequency is optimal.
